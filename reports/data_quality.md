# Data quality report: data/shows.parquet

Generated by `src/analysis/data_quality.py` from the build at 2026-10-19T23:34+00:00. Rerun after `build_shows.py`; every number below is recomputed.

**Bottom line.** 71,285 show rows, of which 24,078 have a price. The **analysis sample is 23,952 rows from 316 artists** (773 tour articles). The sample is big-tour, mostly North American (US 64%), and concentrated in 2008–2019 plus 2022–2025. Prices are clean once the 49 flagged rows are removed (no CPI gaps, no duplicate keys). The biggest risks are **selection** (which shows have figures), **"$" read as USD** at non-US shows, and **features that are ex post or current snapshots**.

## 4. Missingness

![missingness heatmap](charts/dq_missingness_heatmap.png)

Patterns to plan around:
- **Pageview features (`pv_*`) are structurally missing before mid-2015**, so popularity and momentum hypotheses are tested only on 2016+ shows (10,518 rows).
- **`days_announce_to_show` is ~empty** (0.0% non-null). There's no lead-time feature.
- **Metro (Census) features are US-only** (66% of the sample). `is_college_town` is True on only 121 sample rows.
- **Venue Wikidata fields** (type, capacity, coordinates) are missing for the 18.7% of sample shows whose venues haven't been resolved yet, plus venues with no item.
- **Weather** is `ok` for 5,561 sample rows (23%). The rest are pending (rerun) or have no coordinates (non-US venues without a Wikidata point).
- **Bill:** `n_openers` is known for 50% of the sample (a blank opener cell means unknown). Opener and album pageviews are nearly empty (see section 8).

## 5. Duplicates

- `show_id` is unique (71,285 ids; 18 carry a `__2`/`__3` collision suffix), and every feature table is unique on its key. The join is validated many-to-one and never changes the row count (tests/test_shows_join.py).
- **Box office copied across artists:** 25 groups (50 rows) where the same gross at the same venue and date appears in two artists' tour articles (an opener's or co-headliner's article quoting the headliner's Boxscore line, e.g. U2/Garbage, Guns N' Roses/ZZ Top, Kenny Chesney/Jason Aldean). One row per group is kept: the headliner when the opening-act cell names the other artist (7 groups), else the artist with more priced shows. The dropped copies are `dup_boxoffice_drop`.
- **One performer, several artist_ids:** the spine's artist_id is per act as written, so one performer can appear under several ids ("Beyoncé" vs. "Beyoncé, Alicia Keys and Missy Elliott"; "P!nk" vs. "Pink"). In the sample, 30 performer groups span more than one artist_id (5,993 rows). Hashing artist_id would have put 14 performers in two splits, so the split hashes `split_key` (performers linked by QID or headliner wikilink) and artist medians use `artist_key`. **Ask collector-prices whether P!nk/Pink-style aliases should be merged in the spine.**
- **Same artist, date and city at two venues:** 51 rows, almost all unpriced (promo appearances, festival side stages). They're left in.
- **Multi-night aggregates:** 32,046 rows sit in 8,970 aggregate groups. They're treated as one unit each (DATA_DICTIONARY.md, Join decisions). 846 member rows couldn't be placed in a group from row order; they carry no figures, so nothing is lost. 52 priced aggregates span several venues (leg totals) and are excluded.

## 7. Selection bias

![selection by tour position](charts/dq_selection_by_tour_position.png)

- **Which tours:** only tours big enough to report to Billboard Boxscore/Pollstar *and* to have a Wikipedia box office table. These are arena and stadium acts with long careers; the top 10 artists supply 19% of the sample rows. Results describe superstar pricing, not the concert market. Effects that matter for mid-size acts (opener draw, college towns, club tours) are the least identifiable.
- **Which shows within a tour:** the priced share falls from 39% of shows in the first fifth of a tour to 28% in the last fifth. Later legs, and especially non-North-American legs, are less often reported. By continent: north_america 46%, latin_america 32%, oceania 28%, europe 25%, mea 24%, asia 5% priced. By capacity bucket: <3k club/theater 58%, 3k-8k theater/amph. 78%, 8k-25k arena 91%, 25k+ stadium 82%. If reporting correlates with success (promoters report strong grosses), priced shows are skewed toward stronger nights and level estimates are biased up. Within-tour contrasts (weekend, leg) are biased only if reporting depends on that contrast.
- **When:** coverage before 2008 is sparse and older articles are edited less. Era-specific results should be read as "among documented tours".
- **Mitigations for task 6:** use tour and artist fixed effects (identification within selected units), report results by era, compare first-leg-only estimates, and model `P(priced)` from spine features as a diagnostic (rows without figures are kept for exactly this).

## 8. Can the data test each hypothesis?

Power is assessed on the analysis sample, in log points (0.05 ≈ 5%), at 80% power and α = 0.05. Binary variables use only the groups (tours or artists, per the FE named) where the variable varies. Continuous variables are scaled to a 1-SD within-group change.
- **naive MDE** = 2.8 × within-group residual SD × √(1/n₁ + 1/n₀) (binary), or 2.8 × SD / √n (continuous). It treats shows as independent, so it is far too optimistic.
- **clustered MDE** = 2.8 × the cluster-robust SE (clustered by performer group, `split_key`) of the fixed-effects slope, computed on the real data. **The coefficient itself is deliberately not computed or reported here** (that's task 6). This is the planning number. Genre uses artist medians: 2.8 × SD(artist median) × √(2/artists in a median-sized genre).

The real power driver is the number of tours or artists with variation, not n.
