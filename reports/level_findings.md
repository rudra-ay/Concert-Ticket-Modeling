## Method

- Outcome: log average ticket price in Sep-2026 dollars (`log_avg_price_real`; aggregates deflated at their midpoint month). Effects are reported as % = exp(b) − 1; continuous regressors per 1 SD of the regressor in the estimation sample.
- **Every spec includes year FE** plus the FE named (artist = `artist_key`, one per performer; tour = `tour_unit`; performer group = `split_key`), absorbed by alternating projections (`src/analysis/fe.py`, checked against statsmodels in tests/test_fe.py).
- **Tour units (amendment A1, approved):** tour_keys spanning more than 2 years (list articles, residencies, tours with long pauses: Phish concert tours, List of Yes tours, Billy Joel at MSG, Celine, No Filter's COVID break) are split at gaps over 90 days, then by calendar year if a piece still spans more than 2 years. In the analysis sample, 63 tour_keys (3,807 rows) carry a split label, and 53 of them (3,614 rows) have ≥ 2 units inside the sample; the max unit span is 1.87 years. Shorter tours stay whole, so breaks between legs stay within a tour.
- **Standard errors:** cluster-robust (CR1) by `split_key` (performer group, which nests artist and tour). Regressors that vary at metro or venue level (H1b/H1c, H5/H5w/H5o) are **two-way clustered**, split_key × metro or split_key × venue (Cameron–Gelbach–Miller). Inference uses t with **df = G_eff − 1**, where G_eff counts clusters in which the regressor varies within its FE group.
- **Few clusters:** when G_eff or the number of FE groups carrying the variation is below 30, p comes from a null-imposed wild cluster bootstrap (Webb weights, 1,999 draws), and a leave-one-group-out range is reported.
- Sensitivity specs for every headline: US only (`country_final`), excluding "$" outside the US, 2016+, excluding multi-night aggregates, weighted by nights, **inverse-probability weighted for selection** (data/selection_weights.parquet), **tours with ≥ 80% coverage**, and **excluding split pseudo-tours**. Weekend and holiday also get a within-tour metro-size control (US). "same as base" means the restriction is already part of the base spec.
- Multiple testing: Benjamini–Hochberg over a family fixed in advance (next section).
- Leakage labels: EXPOST, OUTCOME, POST_ONSALE and SNAPSHOT regressors appear only in these explanatory regressions, labeled. The predictive model excludes everything coded EXPOST/OUTCOME/TARGET/SHOWDAY/POST_ONSALE/ID.
- City premium (H1a) is a dispersion test: metros with ≥ 30 shows from ≥ 5 artists get dummies (artist + year FE). The effect size is the bias-corrected SD of their premiums, √(var(b) − mean(SE²)); p comes from 999 within-artist permutations on the reduced-model residuals, so 0.001 is the floor.

## Data caveats that bound every finding

- **Selection** (src/analysis/selection.py): 58% of played shows in documented tours are covered. Coverage falls over a tour (within-tour LPM: progress -0.119) and rises with metro size (+0.024 per log point) and venue size. Better-documented tours price slightly higher within an artist's era (+0.043 per unit of tour coverage, SE 0.016). Coverage also varies within tours, so within-tour contrasts don't remove this by construction; the IPW and ≥ 80%-coverage specs test it.
- **Currency** (reports/currency_test.md): within-tour FX slopes show no local-currency contamination. Pooled θ = -0.202, 95% CI [-0.430, +0.025]; CA +0.008; AU -0.463 (the opposite sign from contamination).
- **Superstar sample:** these results describe big Boxscore-reporting tours only.

### F1 [L1]. H1a: City premium: dispersion of metro effects

**Verdict: SUPPORTED (robust).** Base effect SD of metro premiums 10.8 log points; p = 0.0010, q = 0.008; n = 23,314 shows, G = 229 clusters (split_key), G_eff = — clusters with variation (t df = G_eff − 1), 146 FE groups with variation. FE: artist_key + year. Leakage label: none. bias-corrected SD of 146 metro premiums (artist + year FE; reference = other metros); p from 999 within-artist permutations of metro labels on artist+year-FE residuals; raw SD 0.112

Metro premiums are relative to the pooled smaller metros, net of artist and year. **Why the within-artist permutation is valid under metro-level dependence (review 2, P1):** under the null of no metro effects, an artist's shows are exchangeable across its metros given artist and year. A shock common to all artists in a metro *is* a metro effect, so it belongs to the alternative, not to the nuisance structure the null must preserve. The statistic is computed on artist + year-FE residuals, and permutation happens within artist, so the artist's career and calendar structure is kept. The FX test (reports/currency_test.md) finds no local-currency contamination of "$" figures, so non-US metro premiums aren't currency artifacts. Australia's premium does move with the AUD/USD rate, though (θ_AU < 0), so it's era-dependent. The Las Vegas premium likely reflects residency/casino shows and high-roller pricing, not the city alone.

65 of 146 metros differ from the reference at q < 0.05 (BH within metros). Priciest: Singapore, SG +64%, Tokyo, JP +34%, Las Vegas-Henderson-North Las Vegas, NV +33%, Brisbane, AU +28%, Sydney, AU +28%. Cheapest: Warsaw, PL -25%, Lisbon, PT -21%, Batavia, NY -21%, Milan, IT -19%, Virginia Beach-Chesapeake-Norfolk, VA-NC -19%. US-only: SD 7.9 log points (p = 0.001). Chart: charts/level_metros.png.
